# The Wispr Flow Job Portfolio

Thirteen distinct jobs, each written in the standard form:

> **When** *[situation / context]*, **I want to** *[motivation]*, **so I can**
> *[expected outcome]*.

**The discipline:** a different *"when"* means a different job. If two entries
share a circumstance, they are one job written twice. Each is then read
before/after gen AI, and tagged with the force that was actually binding.

---

## The portfolio at a glance

| # | Job | Family | Existed before gen AI? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| J1 | Hand over full context to a model | **Machine-directed** | **No — new** |
| J2 | Redirect a model that's 80% right | **Machine-directed** | **No — new** |
| J3 | Brief an agent on a long task | **Machine-directed** | **No — new** |
| J4 | Clear a backlog of replies | Human-directed | Yes |
| J5 | Get past the blank page | Human-directed | Yes — very old |
| J6 | Capture when hands or eyes are busy | Situational | Yes |
| J7 | Work at full capacity when typing hurts | Situational | Yes — **the only pre-AI market** |
| J8 | Produce real text on a phone | Situational | Yes |
| J9 | Write as fluently as I speak | Fluency | Yes — largely unserved |
| J10 | Think in one language, write in another | Fluency | Yes — unserved |
| J11 | Capture thought before I lose it | Cognitive | Yes |
| J12 | Dictate where others can hear me | **Second-order** | **No — created by the product** |
| J13 | Dictate confidential material | **Second-order** | **No — created by adoption** |

Three families of one. **Only J1–J3 are new** — and they are the growth engine,
because they scale with AI adoption rather than with headcount.

# Family 1 — Machine-directed
### Created by generative AI. Did not exist in 2021.

## J1 — Hand over full context to a model

> **When** I need a model to help me with something that requires real context
> before it can be useful, **I want to** hand over everything in my head without
> pruning it down to what I'm willing to type, **so I can** get a usable answer
> on the first attempt instead of the fourth.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | No. There was nothing to give context *to*. |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | The nearest circumstance was a search box, which punished extra words. We were trained for forty years to **strip language out**. |
| **What changed** | The machine now rewards saying more. Typing is the only thing standing between the context in your head and the context in the prompt. |
| **Binding force** | **Push (new)** — machine-directed prose at volume. **Habit (absent)** — nobody has a typing habit for this. |

**The finding worth a slide:** typing cost was **silently degrading prompt
quality**. People write shorter prompts than they mean to, drop the caveat, skip
the example, omit the constraint — not because they judged it unnecessary but
because it was another thirty seconds of typing. Removing the input tax makes
the prompt longer, and a longer prompt is a better prompt.

> **So Wispr's value partly accrues to a different product.** It makes ChatGPT
> answer better. That is a strange and under-appreciated form of value creation,
> and it's worth asking the room who captures it.

---

## J2 — Redirect a model that's 80% right

> **When** a model's answer is close but wrong in a specific way, **I want to**
> say what's off in the way I'd say it to a colleague, **so I can** converge in
> three turns instead of giving up and accepting something worse.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | No. Nothing was ever 80% right and improvable by conversation. |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | — |
| **What changed** | The interaction became **multi-turn**. Correction became a first-class activity. |
| **Binding force** | **Push (new)** — and it bites hardest here. |

**Why this is the sharpest job in the portfolio:** friction bites hardest on the
**marginal turn**. Turn one is obviously worth the effort. Turn four has
*uncertain* payoff — maybe it fixes it, maybe not. When the marginal turn costs
thirty seconds of typing, people stop early and accept a worse answer. When it
costs five seconds of talking, they keep going.

> **Voice doesn't just make each turn faster. It increases how many turns people
> are willing to take** — and therefore the quality of what they end up with.
>
> This is very likely a real driver behind that ~70% twelve-month retention, and
> it is invisible in any metric that counts words per minute.

---

## J3 — Brief an agent on a long task

> **When** I'm setting an agent off on a multi-step task I won't be watching,
> **I want to** specify the constraints, the edge cases and what *not* to do,
> **so I can** avoid babysitting it or cleaning up after it.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | No — and it barely existed in 2023. This is the *frontier* version. |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | The closest analogue is briefing a junior colleague, which was done **verbally.** |
| **What changed** | Agentic products need long, careful, up-front specification. Getting it wrong is expensive. |
| **Binding force** | **Push, and rising fastest** |

**Note the shape:** the pre-AI analogue of this job was *always spoken.* You
briefed a person by talking to them. Typing an agent brief is the anomaly, not
the norm — which tells you the direction this is heading.

# Family 2 — Human-directed
### Forty years old. Existed, and dictation failed them.

## J4 — Clear a backlog of replies

> **When** I have forty unanswered messages and twenty minutes before my next
> meeting, **I want to** clear them at something near speaking speed, **so I
> can** end the day without a queue hanging over me.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Dictation could produce the words but not something **sendable**. Editing ate the time saved. |
| **What changed** | Output is sendable unedited. The **social job** is finally served. |
| **Binding force** | **Anxiety in use** — "if it comes out badly I've lost the time twice" |

---

## J5 — Get past the blank page

> **When** I need to write something careful and I'm staring at an empty field
> with nothing coming, **I want to** talk my way into it, **so I can** find out
> what I actually think instead of freezing.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes — this one is *ancient* |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Unserved. Old dictation demanded you already have the finished sentence in your head, so it was **useless precisely when you were stuck.** |
| **What changed** | You can start talking mid-thought. The product tolerates you not knowing where the sentence ends. |
| **Binding force** | **Anxiety in choice** — the old tool required a competence you didn't have in this moment |

**This is not a speed job.** It's a *thinking* job. People discover what they
think by saying it. That the same product serves both the fastest job (J4) and
the slowest one (J5) is a good five minutes in class — **speed is not the value
proposition, even though it's the one on the website.**

# Family 3 — Situational

## J6 — Capture when hands or eyes are busy

> **When** I'm walking, cooking, driving or between meetings and something
> occurs to me, **I want to** get it down without stopping what I'm doing, **so
> I can** not lose it.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Voice memos — which **deferred** the work rather than doing it. You still had to transcribe it later, so the capture created a debt. |
| **What changed** | Capture and completion collapse into one step. No debt. |
| **Binding force** | **Push (situational)** — high, but intermittent |

---

## J7 — Work at full capacity when typing hurts

> **When** typing causes me pain or is not fully available to me — RSI,
> arthritis, injury, disability — **I want to** work at my full capacity, **so I
> can** have my output determined by my thinking rather than by my hands.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes, always |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | **This was Dragon's entire market**, and it was served — badly, expensively, but genuinely. |
| **What changed** | The same capability, without the training, the cost, or the poor output. |
| **Binding force** | **Push — extreme.** High enough to overcome everything else. |

**The most important analytical point in this document.** Ask the class: *why
did dictation survive for forty years if nobody hired it?*

Because in **one segment** the Push was so extreme it overwhelmed the Anxiety and
Habit. People for whom typing was painful or unavailable had no alternative, so
they absorbed the enrollment, the training, the cost and the bad output. That
segment kept the category alive.

> **A niche market is often not a segment where the product is unusually good.
> It is a segment where the push is unusually high.**
>
> And that reframes what a "niche" tells you. Dragon's users were not a small
> market — they were **the only people whose forces already resolved.** Everyone
> else wanted the same thing and couldn't justify the cost. When gen AI dropped
> the anxiety, the niche turned out to have been the visible tip of a general
> market all along.

This is the single most transferable idea for students evaluating any category
that has been "niche for decades."

---

## J8 — Produce real text on a phone

> **When** I'm on my phone and need to produce more than a sentence, **I want to**
> not thumb-type it, **so I can** do real work away from a desk.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes — and the push was always *higher* here than on desktop |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Phone dictation shipped free on every handset for a decade. **Still lost** — same cleanup problem. |
| **What changed** | Cleanup |
| **Binding force** | **Anxiety in use** |

**A useful control experiment:** mobile had *more* Push than desktop and *the
same* free solution, and adoption still didn't happen. That isolates the
variable — it was never about how much the typing hurt. **It was always about
what came out.**

# Family 4 — Fluency and cognition

## J9 — Write as fluently as I speak

> **When** I'm writing in a language I speak fluently but write more slowly and
> less confidently, **I want to** produce text that reads as fluent, **so I can**
> be judged on what I said rather than on my grammar.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes, hugely |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Essentially unserved. Grammar checkers caught errors *after* the fact; the anxiety was *before*. |
| **What changed** | The cleanup layer **is** a fluency layer. It doesn't correct your English — it renders your meaning in fluent English. |
| **Binding force** | **Anxiety — social, and severe** |

**Worth dwelling on with an Indian cohort**, because the room will recognise it
immediately. Enormous numbers of people speak a language far more fluently and
confidently than they write it. For them the gap between spoken and written
competence is not a speed problem, it's a **credibility** problem — and it is
felt most acutely in exactly the high-stakes writing that matters most for a
career.

This may be the largest under-discussed segment in the entire category, and it
is almost invisible in a US-centric reading of the product.

---

## J10 — Think in one language, write in another

> **When** the thought arrives in my first language but the output has to be in
> English, **I want to** say it as it comes and get English out, **so I can**
> work at the speed I think rather than the speed I translate.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Unserved. Translation tools required you to first *write* the source. |
| **What changed** | The translation step folds into the same pass as the cleanup |
| **Binding force** | **Push** — translation is real cognitive load |

---

## J11 — Capture thought before I lose it

> **When** thoughts arrive faster than my hands can move and I can feel one
> slipping, **I want to** get it out at the speed it arrived, **so I can** keep
> the thread instead of losing the second idea while recording the first.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | Yes |
| **Pre-gen-AI** | Unserved — old dictation *added* cognitive load by demanding pre-formed sentences |
| **What changed** | You can speak at the speed the thought arrives |
| **Binding force** | **Push** — and it's an *emotional* push (the frustration of losing an idea), not a functional one |

Frequently reported by people who describe themselves as ADHD. Worth naming as a
segment where the emotional job dominates the functional one.

# Family 5 — Second-order
### Created by the product's own success.

## J12 — Dictate where other people can hear me

> **When** I'm in an open-plan office, a café or a shared home, **I want to**
> dictate without disturbing anyone or being overheard, **so I can** use this
> everywhere rather than only when I'm alone.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | **No.** You can't have this problem until you've adopted the thing. |
| **What created it** | Adoption. The product works, so now you want it in places you can't easily talk. |
| **Product response** | Hold-to-talk (not always-listening), and **Canto** — their own speech model for noisy environments, cutting word error in noise from >30% to 5–10% |
| **Binding force** | **Anxiety — social** |

**Canto is not a capability upgrade. It is this job, productised.** The
constraint on the total addressable market was never accuracy in a quiet room.
It was that most work does not happen in a quiet room.

---

## J13 — Dictate confidential material

> **When** the text contains client, patient or commercially sensitive
> information, **I want to** know it isn't retained or trained on, **so I can**
> use this for the work that actually matters rather than only for the trivial
> parts.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Existed before?** | **No.** A prerequisite of using it. |
| **What created it** | Enterprise adoption |
| **Product response** | SOC 2, SSO, enforced HIPAA — **the Enterprise tier is this job with a price on it** |
| **Binding force** | **Anxiety in use** |

# What the portfolio tells you

**1. Only three of thirteen jobs are new — and they're the growth engine.**
J1–J3 scale with AI adoption. Every other job scales with headcount, which grows
a few percent a year. **A portfolio where the new jobs are the fast-growing ones
is what a durable AI-native product looks like.**

**2. The niche was never a niche.** J7 is the only job where Push exceeded
Anxiety pre-gen-AI, which is the entire reason a dictation industry existed at
all. Those users weren't a small market — they were the only people whose forces
already resolved. *A long-standing niche is usually a signal of a blocked general
market, not a small one.*

**3. Speed is not the value proposition,** even though it's the pitch. J5 (the
blank page), J9 (fluency), J11 (losing the thought) and J7 (pain) are not speed
jobs. Several users would use this if it were *slower* than typing.

**4. Mobile is the control experiment.** More push, same free solution, still no
adoption. Isolates cleanup as the variable — J8.

**5. The social job splits the portfolio in half.** J1–J3 have **no** social job:
nobody reads your prompts. J4, J5, J9 have a **severe** one. Same utterance, two
definitions of success — which is why destination-awareness is the product.

**6. Two second-order jobs are already the enterprise motion.** J12 became Canto;
J13 became the Enterprise tier. **Watch which second-order job a company
productises first — that's the clearest signal of where it thinks the money is.**

# Using this in class

Don't present all thirteen. Put the summary table up, then work **three**:

| Job | Why this one |
| --- | --- |
| **J1** | The new job. Lands the whole "gen AI created the push" argument, and the twist that Wispr makes *other* products better. |
| **J7** | The niche reframe. The most transferable idea in the session. |
| **J9** | Lands hardest in an Indian cohort, and shows an emotional/social job the US framing misses entirely. |

**Then the exercise:** hand teams a different product and twenty minutes to build
the same table — jobs, families, existed-before, binding force. The test of
whether they've understood: **do they find any second-order jobs?** Teams that
list only first-order jobs have described the product, not analysed it.
