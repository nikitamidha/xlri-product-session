01 · The one idea

People don’t buy products. They hire them.

McDonald’s wanted to sell more milkshakes. They asked milkshake buyers what would make a better milkshake — thicker, cheaper, chunkier — shipped all of it, and sales did not move.

Then someone asked a different question: what job were these people hiring a milkshake to do? Nearly half of all milkshakes were sold before 8am, to solo drivers, taken away. The job was: “make my boring 30-minute commute less boring, with one hand, without crumbs on my suit, and keep me full until 11.”

The real competitors were a banana (gone in two minutes), a bagel (needs two hands), and a doughnut (crumbs). Not other milkshakes.

A job to be done is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance — functionally, emotionally and socially.

Three consequences, and they are the whole reason the framework is worth your time:

CONSEQUENCE 01

Your competitor set changes. It includes bananas. It includes doing nothing at all.

CONSEQUENCE 02

Your unit of analysis changes. Not a customer — a circumstance. The same person hires different things at 7am and at 4pm.

CONSEQUENCE 03

Every hire is also a fire. Something got displaced. If you cannot name what, you have not found a job.

02 · Definition

A job is not a task, an activity, a feature, or a category.

Often called a jobWhat it actually is
“Dictating”An activity. Nobody wants to dictate.
“Using our app”A solution — and our goal, not theirs.
“Voice input”A category.
“Onboarding”A stage in our funnel.
“Being more productive”An aspiration. Unfalsifiable, so it rules nothing out.

Two tests, run them in this order

TEST 01 · THE STRIKE TEST

Delete every product, brand and technology name from your statement. If what remains still reads as true, you have a job. If the sentence dies, you had a feature.

TEST 02 · THE AGE TEST

Would the statement have been recognisable twenty years ago? Jobs are old. Solutions are new. A “job” that could not have existed before 2023 is almost always a capability in disguise.

A third, blunter test: does the statement kill anything on your roadmap? A job statement that rules nothing out is doing no work.

03 · The form

Three parts. You need all three.

When — the circumstance I have a formed thought and an empty text field in front of me, What makes the job arise? A moment you could have filmed.
I want to — the motivation get it out of my head at something near the speed I think it, The job proper. No product may appear here.
So I can — the outcome stay in the flow of the work instead of becoming my own transcriptionist. The standard they judge success by. Not your metric.

Drop the circumstance and you get a universal truth that describes everyone and directs nothing.

Drop the outcome and you have a slogan — nothing to measure, so nothing to falsify.

Strike “Wispr Flow” from the statement above and it still holds — and would have held in 1985. That is the strike test and the age test passing at the same time. Which raises the real question: if the job is forty years old, why did nobody hire a product for it until 2024?

04 · Depth

Every job has three layers. Most roadmaps only serve one.

LAYER 01

Functional

What is the practical task?

Convert a thought into usable text.

LAYER 02

Emotional

How do I want to feel — or avoid feeling?

Not experience myself as the bottleneck to my own thinking.

LAYER 03

Social

How do I want to be perceived?

Produce text that reads as competent — nobody should be able to tell I dictated it.

Dictation software served the functional layer beautifully for forty years, was given away free with Windows, and was hired by almost nobody. It failed on a layer nobody had written down. Speaking to your laptop in an open-plan office is a social act; sending prose that reads as dictated is a social risk.

The emotional and social layers are where the durable advantage sits, precisely because they are hard to measure and therefore rarely copied on purpose.

05 · Quality control

Good and bad jobs to be done

StatementVerdictWhy
“Our users want a better spreadsheet.”SolutionNames a category. Strike it and nothing remains.
“Millennials want convenience.”DemographicA segment is not a circumstance. Two people in the same segment, in different moments, hire different things.
“I want to be more productive.”UnfalsifiableNo circumstance, no standard of success. Rules nothing out, so it cannot prioritise.
“I want to check my balance in the app.”Our goalFeature usage. The user’s job is knowing whether they can afford the thing they are standing in front of.
“When I’m on the 6:40 train with no signal, I want the day’s numbers already on my phone, so I can walk into the 9am with a point of view.”JobFilmable circumstance, solution-free motivation, checkable outcome. It also rules out a live dashboard.
“When my parents ask what I’m doing with my life, I want an answer that sounds like a plan, so I can stop feeling behind my batchmates.”JobThe emotional and social job of hiring an MBA. No brochure writes it down. Every applicant feels it.

The bad ones share a shape: they describe a person or a product. The good ones describe a moment and a standard.

Useful habit: write the job, then write down two things it forbids you from building. If you cannot, the statement is decoration.

06 · Context

What a good context looks like

The circumstance is the load-bearing half of the job. Get it wrong and everything downstream is a survey result.

01 · SITUATIONAL, NOT PERSONAL

A moment, not a demographic, not a personality. “Busy professionals” → “the fifteen minutes between back-to-back calls”.

02 · FILMABLE

If you could not have pointed a camera at it, it is an attitude, not a circumstance. Where were they? What time? Who else was there?

03 · COUNTABLE

It recurs at a knowable frequency. Frequency × population is your market size — and it is a far better estimate than a category report.

04 · SOLUTION-FREE

No product names, yours or anyone’s. “When I open the app…” is not a context; it is a funnel step.

05 · CONSTRAINING

A good context explains why the obvious answer fails here. “No signal” kills the dashboard. “One hand on the wheel” kills the bagel.

How you actually get it

You do not ask for context. People answer that question with a self-portrait. You reconstruct it, from one real event:

Instead ofAsk
“When do you usually…?”“Take me to the last time. What day was it?”
“Why did you need it?”“What had just happened before you sat down?”
“Where do you use it?”“Where were you physically standing? Was anyone with you?”
“How often?”“How many times did that happen last week?”

The tell that you have the real context: it comes with a time of day, a location, and another person in it. Generic contexts have none of those.

07 · Outcomes

What a good outcome looks like

An outcome statement is the standard the user privately judges you against. Two grammatical forms, and only two.

Minimise the time it takes to [step]
Minimise the likelihood that [bad thing]

CandidateVerdict
“Improve NPS to 60.”✗ Our outcome. The user has never heard of it.
“Delight the user.”✗ Not measurable, not falsifiable.
“Increase daily active users.”✗ For most jobs, less time in your product is the outcome.
“Minimise the time to correct a misheard word.”✓ Measurable, solution-free, stable across technologies.
“Minimise the likelihood the recipient can tell it was dictated.”✓ A social outcome, written as a testable statement.

Five ways to pull outcomes out of a person

01

“How did you know it had worked?” — the answer is an outcome statement, badly worded.

02

“What did you check afterwards?” — whatever they inspected is what they were worried about.

03

Hunt the workaround. Every spreadsheet, sticky note and copy-paste ritual is an unserved outcome wearing a disguise.

04

“Tell me about the last time it let you down.” — failure stories are outcomes stated in the negative.

05

Rewrite every complaint as a “minimise” sentence. If it will not convert, it was a feature request, not an outcome.

08 · Where the opening is

Score every outcome against today’s solutions.

Write out every outcome for one job. Mark each row for how well the current way serves it — not your product, the way they do it today, including doing nothing. This is the whole method for getting from “we understand the user” to “we know what to build.”

Outcome statement — getting a thought into text, before generative AIHow the old way served it
Minimise the time to get a formed thought into a text fieldPartly served
Minimise the effort to structure a thought before expressing itMade actively worse
Minimise the likelihood the output needs editing before it can be sentUnserved
Minimise the likelihood the recipient can tell it was dictatedUnserved
Minimise the time to correct a misrecognised wordPoorly served
Minimise the setup effort before the first useful outputUnserved

Row two is the entire case. Old dictation did not merely fail that outcome — it was worse than typing, because it demanded you compose finished prose in your head and then perform it aloud. That is precisely the labour the job exists to avoid.

The unserved rows are the roadmap. The “made worse” row is the business.

One more column belongs on this table in a real analysis: importance to the user. High importance × poorly served is the opportunity; high importance × well served is table stakes you must match and cannot win on.

09 · The forces of progress

Four forces act on every switch. Two forward, two back.

PUSH + PULL > ANXIETY + HABIT

Read it as a vector sum, not a scorecard. You do not have to win all four. You have to win the total — and the total is read one person at a time.

10 · How to really think about it — part one

Four forces, four different owners.

This is the reframe that changes what a team does on Monday. The forces are not four boxes of equal status. They belong to different people, and only two of them are actually yours.

ForceBelongs toWhat that means for you
PushTheir situationYou cannot create it. You can only find people who already have it — and name it back to them in their own words. Most “bad” segments are just people without push.
PullYour solutionThe only force you own outright — which is exactly why every roadmap and every campaign lives here, and why so many of them fail to move anything.
AnxietyTheir imaginationIt does not have to be true to block the sale. A factually wrong fear stops the switch just as hard as a real one. Arguing with it makes it worse.
HabitTheir pastAlready paid for, invisible, and often part of their identity. Habit is not loyalty — people are held by habits they actively dislike.

Pull has sharply diminishing returns against an entrenched habit. Being thirty per cent better than something automatic and already paid for is worth approximately nothing. Yet pull is where nearly all the effort goes, because it is the force the team controls.

Anxiety and habit are the two levers actually available to a product team. Almost nobody works on them.

11 · How to really think about it — part two

The forces run on different clocks.

Anxiety splits in two, and the split is the useful part. Anxiety in choice asks “will this work for me?” and blocks the purchase. Anxiety in use asks “will it let me down at a bad moment?” and blocks the second purchase. One is a conversion problem; the other is your churn.

AI products carry unusually high anxiety in use, because the output is probabilistic and the user often cannot verify it. Citations, diffs, previews and undo are not features. They are anxiety instruments.

12 · How to really think about it — part three

Find the binding force. Then use the right instrument on it.

Every switch has one force that decides. Working on any of the other three is motion without movement. Score all four for one real person, pick the largest opposing one, and act on that.

IF PUSH IS BINDING

They are not struggling enough to change anything. Do not build. Re-target. Go find the segment already in pain and stop trying to educate the comfortable.

IF PULL IS BINDING

They cannot picture life on the other side. Show, don’t claim. A thirty-second demo with their own data beats any list of benefits. Midjourney’s early problem was not quality — nobody could imagine the output.

IF ANXIETY IS BINDING

Benefits do not answer fear. Evidence, reversibility and a small first step do: no card required, undo, a preview before it commits, someone like them saying it worked, and a first useful result inside a minute.

IF HABIT IS BINDING

Two moves only. Fit the old groove — same shortcut, same file, import everything. Or catch a broken habit: new job, new laptop, new city, new baby, new financial year. Life events unstick people; that is a targeting instruction, not a platitude.

New circumstances have no incumbent habit — which makes them a land grab. Twenty-five years of typing is entrenched for email. For prompting an AI, the habit is two years old and still forming. That is why the same product can be immovable in one context and obvious in another.

13 · Worked example

Same job, same product idea, forty years apart.

The job — get a formed thought out of my head at the speed I think it — did not change. Dictation software was free with Windows for decades and was hired by nearly nobody. Score the forces and the mystery dissolves.

ForceBefore generative AIAfterWhat moved
PushModerate I think faster than I type — but I only typed as much as I had to say to other humans.Very strong Machine-directed prose, at volume, dozens of times a day.A new circumstance that did not exist in 2021 — and it grows as the models get more useful.
PullModerate “Speech is three times faster.” True, theoretical, heard it before.Very strong Not just faster — finished. And conversation wants to be spoken.The promise became imaginable.
AnxietyVery strong Voice enrollment, a custom dictionary, garbage output, talking at my laptop in an open-plan office.Collapsed The same technology that understands the instruction cleans up the speech.Setup cost and social risk both went to near zero.
HabitVery strong Twenty-five years of typing, costing no conscious thought.Weak — in the new context There is no twenty-five-year habit of typing prompts at an AI.The new circumstance arrived without an incumbent.

Push + Pull < Anxiety + Habit → no switch. For forty years. Real demand, a working solution, free distribution — and no hires, because the forces never resolved.

Note which force the popular account misses. “AI made transcription good” only explains the anxiety row — and cannot explain the timing, because that technology existed two years before the product landed. The push row is the market observation; the anxiety row is only a capability observation.

14 · What to talk about

You do not research a market. You reconstruct one switch.

The unit of study

One person, one real change, recently made. Not a prospect. Not an opinion. Not a segment. An event that actually happened, with a date on it, that you can walk backwards through minute by minute.

WHO TO TALK TO

People who switched in the last few weeks — memory of a decision decays fast. Then, and this is the underused half: people who looked hard and did not switch. That is the cleanest read on anxiety and habit you will ever get.

HOW MANY

You are not counting, you are reconstructing. Eight to twelve interviews on one switch usually saturates — you start predicting the answers.

WHO ELSE WAS IN THE ROOM

Interview everyone in the decision: the spouse, the budget holder, the colleague who vetoed the first option. Most switches are joint decisions pretending to be individual ones.

Walk the timeline backwards

Start at the moment of purchase and reverse out of it. Then walk forward again and check for gaps — the gaps are where the interesting things are hidden.

Hunt for energy spikes. Where did they spend disproportionate effort? Drove across town. Built a comparison spreadsheet at 11pm. Called three friends. Took a half-day off. Argued about it at dinner.

Energy marks the struggling moment. That is where the job lives. Everything else is context around it.

Ask what got fired. Every hire displaces something. And ask what physically happened to the old thing — thrown out, sold, still in a drawer “just in case”. Where the old solution ended up tells you how complete the switch really was.

Play detective, not therapist. You are reconstructing an event, not exploring a feeling — the feelings will arrive on their own, attached to facts, which is the only form in which they are useful.

15 · How to dig

Questions that get you fiction, and the ones that don’t.

Do not askAsk insteadBecause
“What features would you like?”“Walk me through the day you bought it. What time did you get up?”People are excellent witnesses and terrible designers.
“Why did you choose us?”“What else were you looking at — and what ruled each one out?”“Why” invites a rationalisation, constructed on the spot and delivered with total confidence.
“How satisfied are you?”“Tell me about the last time it let you down. What did you do?”Satisfaction is a number. Behaviour is evidence.
“Would you pay for X?”“What did you actually pay for — and what did you stop paying for?”Hypothetical willingness to pay is the most reliably wrong number in product management.
“Do you like it?”“What did you fire to make room for this?”Names the real competitor — which is often “nothing”.
“What’s your biggest problem?”“When did you first think there might be a better way? What made you think it?”Finds first thought, which is usually months or years before the purchase.
“How do you usually do this?”“Show me how you did it last Tuesday.”“Usually” produces an idealised average that describes no actual day.
TECHNIQUE 01

Use silence. Count to five after they stop. The second answer is the true one.

TECHNIQUE 02

Keep their words. Verbatims, not your categories. If your notes read like a summary, the data is already gone.

TECHNIQUE 03

Replay it back wrong, slightly. “So you decided in March?” People correct errors with far more detail than they volunteer.

16 · Gotchas, part one

Six ways to write a job statement that does nothing.

GOTCHA 01

The job is a solution

Tell: strike the product noun and the sentence dies.
Fix: ask “so they can what?” until you reach progress, then delete everything above it.

GOTCHA 02

The job is a task

Tell: it is a verb the user would never volunteer — “dictating”, “filing”, “onboarding”.
Fix: tasks are steps inside a job. Climb one level and check the age test.

GOTCHA 03

Altitude sickness

Tell: “be more productive”, “grow my business”. True of everyone, useful to no one.
Fix: a job statement must forbid something. Name two features it kills.

GOTCHA 04

A persona in the “when”

Tell: “When a busy mid-market sales manager…”
Fix: replace the person with a moment. Circumstances buy things; demographics do not.

GOTCHA 05

Functional only

Tell: the roadmap is entirely speed and cost.
Fix: write the emotional and social layers explicitly. Forty years of dictation software served the functional job perfectly and was never hired.

GOTCHA 06

One job per product

Tell: a single heroic job statement on a slide, and nothing else.
Fix: real products serve a portfolio of jobs, and different jobs are blocked by different forces. Write the portfolio; tag each job with its binding force.

17 · Gotchas, part two

Six ways to run the method and still learn nothing.

GOTCHA 07

Asking instead of reconstructing

Tell: your research is in the future tense — “would you…”, “how likely are you to…”.
Fix: past tense, one real event, one real date.

GOTCHA 08

Workshop JTBD

Tell: beautiful job statements, zero verbatims, written in a room with no customers in it.
Fix: every job statement carries a reference to the interview that produced it — or it is a hypothesis, and must be labelled as one.

GOTCHA 09

Your pull filed as their push

Tell: the push column contains your value proposition.
Fix: push must be quotable from a customer, in the past tense, about their own life. If you wrote it, it is pull.

GOTCHA 10

Answering anxiety with pull

Tell: the reply to “what if it doesn’t work for me?” is another benefit, louder.
Fix: evidence, reversibility, a small first step. Fear is not a shortage of reasons.

GOTCHA 11

Competitors are logos

Tell: the market map contains only companies.
Fix: add non-consumption. For most jobs the largest competitor is not doing the thing at all — and in new markets it is the entire segment being converted.

GOTCHA 12

Big hire, no little hire

Tell: signups look healthy, week-four retention does not.
Fix: the big hire is the purchase; the little hire is choosing it again on Tuesday morning. Two different force readings. Most teams only measure the first.

And one meta-gotcha: three schools travel under the same name — Christensen’s theory of progress, Moesta and Spiek’s switch interviews and forces, Ulwick’s outcome-driven innovation. All three are useful. Blended silently, they produce an analysis that cannot be argued with because nobody can tell what it claims. Say which one you are using.

18 · The assignment

Interview the person sitting next to you.

Subject: why they hired an MBA. Twenty minutes. It is the one switch everyone in this room has genuinely made, recently, at real cost.

What to bring back

01

The timeline: first thought (when did this first cross their mind — probably years ago), passive looking, the event that made it active, the event that forced the decision.

02

Three energy spikes. Where did they spend effort out of proportion to the step?

03

The job statement, all three layers — and be honest about the social one.

04

All four forces, scored, with a verbatim quote for each. Then name the binding force.

05

What they fired — the job they turned down, the city they left, the relationship they put on hold.

What you will find

The functional job on every application form is “acquire skills and a network to change career track”. It is true, and it is almost never the binding force.

The binding force is usually anxiety — two years of forgone salary, and the fear of arriving on the other side no more employable than before — or habit: a job that was survivable, a city they knew, a salary that arrived every month.

And the push almost always has a date on it. A promotion that went to someone else. A batchmate’s LinkedIn post. A restructuring. Find the date and you have found the push.

Then do the same exercise on the product you are working on. If you cannot get a date, a location and another person into the push, you do not yet have one.

If you read one thing

Read Demand-Side Sales 101. It is the least academic of the five and the only one written from inside the interview.

The whole framework in one sentence, to leave the room with:

Find someone who already has a push you did not create, in a circumstance you can film, and remove enough anxiety and habit that they can afford to try.
