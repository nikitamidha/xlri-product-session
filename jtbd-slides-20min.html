The switch.

Jobs to Be Done, in one sitting. Nobody wants your product. They want the progress it makes possible — and they will fire it the moment something else makes that progress cheaper.

01 · The one idea

People don’t buy products. They hire them.

McDonald’s asked milkshake buyers what would make a better milkshake — thicker, cheaper, chunkier — shipped all of it, and sales did not move.

Then someone asked what job the milkshake was being hired to do. Nearly half were sold before 8am, to solo drivers, taken away. The job: “make my boring commute less boring, with one hand, no crumbs on my suit, and keep me full until 11.”

The real competitors were a banana, a bagel and a doughnut. Not other milkshakes.

A job to be done is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance — functionally, emotionally and socially.

CONSEQUENCE 01

Your competitor set changes. It includes bananas. It includes doing nothing at all — which is the biggest competitor most products have.

CONSEQUENCE 02

Your unit of analysis changes. Not a customer — a circumstance. The same person hires different things at 7am and at 4pm.

CONSEQUENCE 03

Every hire is a fire. Something got displaced. If you cannot name what, you have not found a job.

02 · The form

Three parts, and three layers.

When — the circumstance I’m at a vegetable cart, the bill is ₹143 and I have a ₹500 note, What makes the job arise? A moment you could have filmed.
I want to — the motivation settle the exact amount then and there, The job proper. No product may appear here.
So I can — the outcome walk away without owing anyone, or being owed, or taking a toffee as change. The standard they judge success by. Not your metric.
LAYER 01 · FUNCTIONAL

Move the exact amount from me to them.

LAYER 02 · EMOTIONAL

Not feel cheated over ₹7, and not feel like the person making a fuss over ₹7.

LAYER 03 · SOCIAL

Not be the customer who holds up the queue. And on the vendor’s side: not be the shop that turns people away for want of change.

Strike every product name and it still holds — and would have held in 1985. That is the strike test and the age test passing together. Jobs are old; solutions are new. If your “job” could not have existed before 2023, you have found a capability, not a job.

03 · Quality control

Good and bad jobs to be done

StatementVerdict
“Our users want a better fitness app.”Solution Names a category. Strike it and nothing remains.
“Gen Z wants instant gratification.”Demographic A segment is not a circumstance.
“I want to manage my money better.”Unfalsifiable Rules nothing out, so it cannot prioritise.
“I want to finish KYC in the app.”Our goal A compliance step of ours, endured by them.
“When I’m splitting a dinner bill six ways and everyone’s doing mental arithmetic, I want to settle before we leave the table, so nobody has to chase anyone next week.”Job Filmable, solution-free, checkable — and it rules out a shared-expense ledger.
“When my parents ask what I’m doing with my life, I want an answer that sounds like a plan, so I can stop feeling behind my batchmates.”Job The real job of hiring an MBA. No brochure writes it down.

The test that matters

Does the statement kill anything on your roadmap? A job statement that rules nothing out is doing no work.

Weak: “Field sales want a better CRM.” Kills nothing — the dashboard, the redesign, the reports module all survive.

Strong: “When I’m walking out of one client meeting to the next, I want what just happened recorded before I forget it, so I’m not reconstructing my week on Friday night.”

Kills the desktop-first redesign, the analytics module, and mandatory-field validation. Funds two-tap capture, offline queue, and pre-filling from the calendar entry. The constraints in the circumstance do the killing — not sharper wording.

04 · The two halves that do the work

A circumstance you could film. A standard you could check.

What makes a context good

Situational, not personal. “Busy professionals” → “the fifteen minutes between back-to-back calls”.

Filmable. A time of day, a location, another person in it. Generic contexts have none of those.

Countable. Frequency × population is your market size — and a better estimate than any category report.

Constraining. It explains why the obvious answer fails here. “No signal” kills the live dashboard. “One hand on the wheel” kills the bagel.

You never ask for context — people answer that with a self-portrait. You reconstruct it: “take me to the last time; what day was it; what had just happened?”

What makes an outcome good

Minimise the time it takes to [step]
Minimise the likelihood that [bad thing]

Then score every outcome against the way they do it today. Paying for small things in cash, before 2016:

OutcomeCash served it
Minimise the time to pay ₹40 at a tea stallServed
Minimise the likelihood I need exact changeMade worse
Minimise the effort to prove I paidUnserved
Minimise trips to an ATMPoorly served
Minimise the effort to pay someone not in front of meUnserved

Unserved rows are the roadmap. The “made worse” row is the business. NPS, DAU and MRR are your outcomes; the user has never heard of them.

05 · The forces of progress

Four forces act on every switch. Two forward, two back.

PUSH + PULL > ANXIETY + HABIT

A vector sum, not a scorecard. You do not have to win all four — you have to win the total, and the total is read one person at a time.

06 · How to really think about it

The four forces belong to four different people.

Only one of them is yours — which is exactly why most roadmaps push on it and nothing moves.

PUSH · THEIR SITUATION

You cannot create it. You can only find people who already have it and name it back in their words. If push is the binding force, do not build — re-target.

PULL · YOUR SOLUTION

The only force you own outright. Show, don’t claim — thirty seconds with their own data beats any list of benefits. But pull has sharply diminishing returns against an entrenched habit: being 30% better than something automatic and free is worth nothing.

ANXIETY · THEIR IMAGINATION

It does not have to be true to block the sale. Benefits do not answer fear. Evidence, reversibility and a small first step do: no card required, undo, a preview before it commits, someone like them saying it worked.

HABIT · THEIR PAST

Not loyalty — people are held by habits they dislike. Two moves: fit the old groove (same gesture, same file, import everything), or catch a broken habit — new job, new city, new phone, new baby.

Anxiety and habit are the two levers actually available to a product team. Almost nobody works on them.

And anxiety splits in two. Anxiety in choice — “will this work for me?” — blocks the purchase, so it is a conversion problem. Anxiety in use — “will it let me down at a bad moment?” — blocks the second purchase. That one is your churn.

07 · Worked example

UPI: the same product, three readings.

UPI launched in April 2016 with RBI backing, twenty-one banks, and no fee. By the end of October it had done about a lakh of transactions in total. A working solution, free distribution, real demand — and anxiety and habit won.

On 8 November the incumbent was removed. Not by a campaign. By a policy nobody at NPCI could have manufactured — which is the cleanest demonstration you will ever see that push belongs to the situation, not to you.

And the note ban did not hand the win to UPI. The first beneficiary was the wallet already sitting on people’s phones. In a panic, demand goes to whatever has the lowest anxiety and lowest habit cost at that moment — not to the better architecture.

UPI won over the next three years by removing what was left: money stays in your bank, no top-up, any app to any bank, a QR code at every cart, and still no fee. That is the little hire — being chosen again on Tuesday morning.

08 · Gotchas

Six ways to run this and learn nothing.

GOTCHA 01

The job is a solution

Tell: strike the product noun and the sentence dies.
Fix: ask “so they can what?” until you reach progress.

GOTCHA 02

A persona in the “when”

Tell: “When a busy mid-market sales manager…”
Fix: replace the person with a moment. Circumstances buy things; demographics do not.

GOTCHA 03

Altitude sickness

Tell: it rules nothing out.
Fix: run it against your real roadmap. If it kills less than a third, it is decoration.

GOTCHA 04

Functional only

Tell: the roadmap is entirely speed and cost.
Fix: write the emotional and social layers down. They are where the durable advantage sits, precisely because they are hard to measure.

GOTCHA 05

Asking, not reconstructing

Tell: your research is in the future tense — “would you…”, “how likely are you to…”.
Fix: past tense, one real event, one real date. People are good witnesses and bad designers.

GOTCHA 06

Answering anxiety with pull

Tell: the reply to “what if it doesn’t work for me?” is another benefit, louder.
Fix: evidence, reversibility, a small first step. Fear is not a shortage of reasons.

Two more worth naming: a market map with only logos on it has forgotten non-consumption — usually the largest competitor. And counting the big hire (the signup) while ignoring the little hire (choosing it again on Tuesday) is how a product with healthy activation and dead week-four retention gets called a success.

09 · Twenty minutes, with the person next to you

Why did you hire an MBA?

It is the one switch everyone in this room has genuinely made, recently, at real cost. Interview each other and bring back five things — and obey one rule.

01

The timeline — first thought, and the two events that moved it.

02

Three energy spikes: where did they spend effort out of proportion?

03

The job, all three layers — honestly on the social one.

04

All four forces, with a verbatim quote each. Name the binding one.

05

What they fired — the job turned down, the city left.

06

Never ask why. Ask when, where were you, what happened just before.

The functional job on every application form is “acquire skills and a network to change track”. True, and almost never the binding force. The binding force is usually anxiety — two years of forgone salary and the fear of arriving no more employable — or habit: a survivable job, a known city, a salary that arrived every month.

The push almost always has a date on it. A promotion that went to someone else. A batchmate’s post. A restructuring. Find the date and you have found the push.

The whole framework in one sentence: find someone who already has a push you did not create, in a circumstance you can film, and remove enough anxiety and habit that they can afford to try.
