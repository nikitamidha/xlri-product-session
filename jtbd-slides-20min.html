The switch.

Jobs to Be Done, in one sitting. Nobody wants your product. They want the progress it makes possible — and they will fire it the moment something else makes that progress cheaper.

01 · The one idea

People don’t buy products. They hire them.

McDonald’s asked milkshake buyers what would make a better milkshake — thicker, cheaper, chunkier — shipped all of it, and sales did not move.

Then someone asked what job the milkshake was being hired to do. Nearly half were sold before 8am, to solo drivers, taken away. The job: “make my boring commute less boring, with one hand, no crumbs on my suit, and keep me full until 11.”

The real competitors were a banana, a bagel and a doughnut. Not other milkshakes.

A job to be done is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance — functionally, emotionally and socially.

CONSEQUENCE 01

Your competitor set changes. It includes bananas. It includes doing nothing at all — which is the biggest competitor most products have.

CONSEQUENCE 02

Your unit of analysis changes. Not a customer — a circumstance. The same person hires different things at 7am and at 4pm.

CONSEQUENCE 03

Every hire is a fire. Something got displaced. If you cannot name what, you have not found a job.

In the wild

Snickers vs Milky WaySame shelf, same company, different jobs. Snickers is hired to shut hunger up until dinner, so it competes with a banana and a samosa. Milky Way is hired to reward you, so it competes with ice cream.

ZerodhaThe competitor was never another broker. For most Indian households the incumbent was a fixed deposit, gold, or not investing at all — non-consumption, which appears on no market map.

Netflix by postWhat got fired was not the video store’s catalogue. It was the drive back to return the disc, and the late fee waiting if you didn’t.

02 · The form

Three parts, and three layers.

When — the circumstance I’m at a vegetable cart, the bill is ₹143 and I have a ₹500 note, What makes the job arise? A moment you could have filmed.
I want to — the motivation settle the exact amount then and there, The job proper. No product may appear here.
So I can — the outcome walk away without owing anyone, or being owed, or taking a toffee as change. The standard they judge success by. Not your metric.
LAYER 01 · FUNCTIONAL

Move the exact amount from me to them.

LAYER 02 · EMOTIONAL

Not feel cheated over ₹7 — and not feel like the person making a fuss over ₹7.

LAYER 03 · SOCIAL

Not be the customer holding up the queue. On the vendor’s side: not be the shop that turns people away for want of change.

Strike every product name and it still holds — and would have held in 1985. That is the strike test and the age test passing together. Jobs are old; solutions are new.

The social layer, elsewhere

LinkedIn PremiumFunctionally it is InMail and who-viewed-your-profile. Socially it is looking like someone in demand rather than someone looking — which is why the badge is public and the search history is not.

CREDThe functional job — paying a credit card bill — was already free and boring. What was sold was membership: visible proof you belong to the set that pays in full.

The wedding photographerFunctionally, photographs. Socially, evidence for a specific set of relatives that the thing was done properly. Nobody prices it as a camera rental.

03 · Quality control

Good and bad jobs to be done

StatementVerdict
“Our users want a better fitness app.”Solution Names a category. Strike it and nothing remains.
“Gen Z wants instant gratification.”Demographic A segment is not a circumstance.
“I want to manage my money better.”Unfalsifiable Rules nothing out, so it cannot prioritise.
“I want to finish KYC in the app.”Our goal A compliance step of ours, endured by them.
“When I’m splitting a dinner bill six ways and everyone’s doing mental arithmetic, I want to settle before we leave the table, so nobody has to chase anyone next week.”Job What Splitwise and UPI are both hired for — and it rules out a shared-expense ledger nobody settles.
“When my parents ask what I’m doing with my life, I want an answer that sounds like a plan, so I can stop feeling behind my batchmates.”Job The real job of hiring an MBA. No brochure writes it down; every applicant feels it.

The test that matters

Does the statement kill anything on your roadmap? A job statement that rules nothing out is doing no work.

Weak: “Field sales want a better CRM.” Kills nothing — the dashboard, the redesign and the reports module all survive.

Strong: “When I’m walking out of one client meeting to the next, I want what just happened recorded before I forget it, so I’m not reconstructing my week on Friday night.”

Kills the desktop-first redesign, the analytics module and mandatory-field validation. Funds two-tap capture, an offline queue, and pre-filling from the calendar entry. The constraints in the circumstance do the killing — not sharper wording.

Statements that killed something

Netflix“When I’m on a commute with no data” kills the always-streaming assumption the whole product was built on. Downloads shipped in 2016, and the markets that needed them most were not the US.

Zoom“When someone outside my company needs to join in ten seconds” kills the mandatory account signup every competitor required. One link, no login — that single constraint is most of the product.

UPI“When the bill is ₹143 at a cart” kills the card machine: a terminal, a minimum ticket size, a fee and a connection. A printed QR code survives all four constraints.

04 · The two halves that do the work

A circumstance you could film. A standard you could check.

What makes a context good

Situational, not personal. “Busy professionals” → “the fifteen minutes between back-to-back calls”.

Filmable. A time of day, a location, another person in it. Generic contexts have none of those.

Countable. Frequency × population is your market size — and a better estimate than any category report.

Constraining. It explains why the obvious answer fails here. “No signal” kills the live dashboard. “One hand on the wheel” kills the bagel.

You never ask for context — people answer that with a self-portrait. You reconstruct it: “take me to the last time; what day was it; what had just happened?”

What makes an outcome good

Minimise the time it takes to [step]
Minimise the likelihood that [bad thing]

Then score every outcome against the way they do it today. Paying for small things in cash, before 2016:

OutcomeCash served it
Minimise the time to pay ₹40 at a tea stallServed
Minimise the likelihood I need exact changeMade worse
Minimise the effort to prove I paidUnserved
Minimise trips to an ATMPoorly served
Minimise the effort to pay someone not in front of meUnserved

Unserved rows are the roadmap. The “made worse” row is the business. NPS, DAU and MRR are your outcomes; the user has never heard of them.

In the wild

Tatkal on IRCTCThe context is a forty-second window against lakhs of people at 10am. That kills “make the booking form nicer” and funds saved passenger lists and a payment method already loaded before the window opens.

A delivery rider’s appHelmet on, one hand, sun on the screen, engine running. Constraints that kill dense dashboards and fund enormous tap targets, audio callouts, and a screen readable at a glance.

Amazon returns“Minimise the likelihood I’m stuck with something that doesn’t fit” is an outcome, not a feature request. Free returns is one answer to it; so is a better size chart. The outcome names the bar, not the build.

05 · The forces of progress

Four forces act on every switch. Two forward, two back.

PUSH + PULL > ANXIETY + HABIT
The binding force

Zoom — pushThe product shipped in 2013 and was already good. Nothing changed about it in March 2020. The situation changed, and nobody at Zoom manufactured that.

Warby Parker — anxietyBuying spectacles you have never worn. Home Try-On sent five frames for five days, free both ways. They retired it in 2025 — once stores and virtual try-on carried the same load, the scaffolding came down.

WhatsApp — habitYour phone number was the account and your address book was the contact list. Nothing to learn, nothing to rebuild. It did not beat the habit; it moved into it.

06 · How to really think about it

The four forces belong to four different people.

Only one of them is yours — which is exactly why most roadmaps push on it and nothing moves.

PUSH · THEIR SITUATION

You cannot create it — only find people who already have it. If push is binding, do not build. Re-target.

Airbnb did not create demand for a stranger’s spare room. A design conference in San Francisco sold out every hotel in the city, and the founders rented air mattresses to the overflow.

PULL · YOUR SOLUTION

The only force you own outright. Show, don’t claim. But pull has sharply diminishing returns against habit: being 30% better than something automatic and free is worth nothing.

Dropbox answered “what is it?” with a screencast rather than a paragraph, because file sync was not describable — only demonstrable.

ANXIETY · THEIR IMAGINATION

It does not have to be true to block the sale. Benefits do not answer fear. Evidence, reversibility and a small first step do.

Ola and Uber put the driver’s name, photo, number plate and a shareable live trip on the screen. None of it makes the ride better. All of it makes getting into a stranger’s car survivable.

HABIT · THEIR PAST

Not loyalty — people are held by habits they dislike. Two moves: fit the old groove, or catch a habit already broken — new job, new city, new phone, new baby.

Google Sheets opens and saves .xlsx. Apple ships Migration Assistant. Neither asks you to change; both remove the cost of arriving.

Two consequences

Anxiety and habit are the only two levers you actually holdPush belongs to their situation and pull runs out of road. Almost every roadmap works on pull anyway, because it is the one the team controls.

Anxiety splits in two, and the split is the useful partIn choice — “will this work for me?” — blocks the purchase, so it is a conversion problem. In use — “will it let me down at a bad moment?” — blocks the second purchase. That one is your churn.

07 · Worked example

UPI: the same product, three readings.

UPI launched in April 2016 with RBI backing, twenty-one banks and no fee. By the end of October it had done about a lakh of transactions in total. A working solution, free distribution, real demand — and anxiety and habit won.

On 8 November the incumbent was removed. Not by a campaign — by a policy nobody at NPCI could have manufactured.

And the note ban did not hand the win to UPI. The first beneficiary was the wallet already sitting on people’s phones. In a panic, demand goes to whatever has the lowest anxiety and lowest habit cost at that moment — not to the better architecture.

UPI won over the next three years by removing what was left: money stays in your bank, no top-up, any app to any bank, a QR code at every cart, still no fee. That is the little hire — being chosen again on Tuesday morning.

The same shape elsewhere

Zoom, 2020Identical structure. A good product with weak push for seven years; an event nobody manufactured removed the incumbent — the office — overnight. And the winner was not the best conferencing tool. It was the one you could join without making an account.

Netflix vs BlockbusterPush: the trip back and the late fee. Anxiety: “what if I want to watch something tonight?” Netflix removed the trip first and the waiting only later — in that order, because that was the order the forces bound in.

08 · Gotchas

Six ways to run this and learn nothing.

GOTCHA 01

The job is a solution

Tell: strike the product noun and the sentence dies.
Fix: ask “so they can what?” until you reach progress.

Levitt: nobody wants a quarter-inch drill, they want a quarter-inch hole. Push again: they want the shelf up before the in-laws arrive.

GOTCHA 02

A persona in the “when”

Tell: “When a busy mid-market sales manager…”
Fix: replace the person with a moment.

King Charles III and Ozzy Osbourne: both born in Britain in 1948, both married twice, both wealthy, both famous, both lived in castles. Identical on every field on your persona slide. They never hired the same thing.

GOTCHA 03

Altitude sickness

Tell: it rules nothing out.
Fix: run it against your real roadmap. If it kills less than a third, it is decoration.

“Improve the customer experience” has been a quarterly objective almost everywhere, and has never once killed a feature.

GOTCHA 04

Functional only

Tell: the roadmap is entirely speed and cost.
Fix: write the emotional and social layers down.

Google Glass worked — photographs, directions, answers. It died on the social layer, and there is no functional fix for being the person wearing it.

GOTCHA 05

Asking, not reconstructing

Tell: your research is in the future tense.
Fix: past tense, one real event, one real date.

Roughly 200,000 people preferred New Coke in blind tests. It launched on 23 April 1985 and was withdrawn 79 days later. The tests asked about a sip. Nobody asked what Coke was hired for.

GOTCHA 06

Answering anxiety with pull

Tell: the reply to “what if it doesn’t work for me?” is another benefit, louder.
Fix: evidence, reversibility, a small first step.

Electric cars answered range anxiety with acceleration figures for a decade. What moved it was charger maps, battery warranties and an honest range readout.

And finally

Two more worth namingA market map with only logos on it has forgotten non-consumption, usually the largest competitor. And counting the big hire while ignoring the little hire is how gym memberships get sold in January and a product with healthy signups and dead week-four retention gets called a success.

The whole framework in one sentenceFind someone who already has a push you did not create, in a circumstance you can film, and remove enough anxiety and habit that they can afford to try.
