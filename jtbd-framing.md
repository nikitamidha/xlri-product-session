# Jobs to be Done in the AI-Native World

The analytical spine of the session. Everything else in this repo sits on top of
this document.

**What this argues:** Christensen was right that jobs are stable — and that fact
is what makes generative AI legible. If you accept that the job doesn't change,
you are forced to look at what *did* change, and the answer turns out to be
precise, small, and enormously consequential.

---

# Part A — The discipline we're holding to

Stating the rules first, because "jobs to be done" gets used loosely and the
whole value here comes from using it strictly.

## The two canonical forms

**The job story** (situation → motivation → expected outcome):

> **When** *[circumstance]*, **I want to** *[motivation]*, **so I can**
> *[expected outcome]*.

**The outcome statement** (Ulwick's ODI form — direction + metric + object +
context):

> **Minimize the time it takes to** *[step]* — or —
> **Minimize the likelihood that** *[undesirable state]*.

A job statement without an outcome statement is a slogan. Every job below gets
both.

## Four rules we will not break

1. **A job is not an activity and not a solution.** "Dictating" is an activity.
   "Wispr Flow" is a solution. The job is the progress the person is trying to
   make. If your job statement contains a product category, rewrite it.
2. **The job has three layers.** Functional, emotional, social. Most product
   failures are failures at the layers nobody wrote down.
3. **The competitive set includes non-consumption.** The largest competitor is
   very often *not doing the thing at all*, and it never appears in a market map.
4. **Progress is blocked by forces, not by features.** Push, Pull, Anxiety,
   Habit. A better product that doesn't move those four numbers gets no hires.

# Part B — What generative AI actually changes

## B0. Start here: which element of the job statement moved?

The job story has three elements. Ask of each: did it move?

| Element | Did it move? | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Motivation** — *"I want to…"* | **No.** | This is the job proper. It is as old as the work itself. Christensen holds. |
| **Circumstance** — *"When…"* | **Mostly no, for first-order jobs.** But AI **manufactures new circumstances**, and those generate genuinely new jobs. | Stable at the first order, generative at the second. |
| **Expected outcome** — *"so I can…"* | **Yes — this is what moves.** The bar for what counts as success ratchets up. | **The outcome is where the AI-native shift actually lives.** |

If students take one structural idea from the session, this is it:

> **The job didn't change. The acceptable outcome did.**
>
> What counted as success in 2019 — "a transcript I can edit" — is a failure in
> 2026. The motivation is identical. The threshold moved.

Everything below is elaboration of that.

## B1. What changed is hireability, not the job

The job existed. A solution existed. The hire never happened.

That is not a story about capability. It is a story about the **forces of
progress** — and generative AI acts almost entirely on one of them.

| Force | What AI did to it |
| --- | --- |
| **Push** of the situation | ~Unchanged. The pain was always there. |
| **Pull** of the new solution | Increased — but this is where every incumbent was already competing. |
| **Anxiety** of the new solution | **Collapsed.** No training, no setup, no new skill, no new vocabulary, low cost of a failed attempt. |
| **Habit** of the present | Weakened, mostly by social proof rather than by the product. |

> **The under-taught move: most AI-native winners won on Anxiety, not on Pull.**
>
> Their competitors spent decades increasing Pull — more accuracy, more features,
> more capability. The winner reduced the cost of trying and the cost of being
> wrong. Ask of any AI product: *which force is this roadmap actually moving?*

## B2. Products climb the job map

Ulwick's insight: a job decomposes into steps, and a product serves some subset.
Pre-AI, products served **one or two steps**. Post-AI, products absorb **the
steps on either side**.

Take the universal job map against "get a thought into a machine":

| Job step | Pre-gen-AI: who does it | Post-gen-AI: who does it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Define** — decide what to say | User | **User** — always, and this never moves |
| **Prepare** — structure it into sentences | **User, in their head, before speaking** | **Product** |
| **Confirm** — check it's ready | User | User |
| **Execute** — produce the text | User (typing) or system (ASR) | System |
| **Monitor** — spot errors | User, reading closely | User, reading lightly |
| **Modify** — fix what's wrong | **User, heavily** | **Product first, user lightly** |
| **Conclude** — format and place it | **User** | **Product, destination-aware** |

**Four of seven steps moved from the user to the product.** That is the
AI-native shift, expressed as something you can audit rather than assert.

**The exercise:** draw this table for any product. The steps that moved are the
value created. The steps that didn't move are the roadmap.

## B3. The competitor being displaced is usually not a product

JTBD says non-consumption is a competitor. In AI-native markets it is *the*
market.

| Product | Displacing a competitor product? | Actually displacing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wispr Flow | Not Dragon | **Typing**, and *not writing it down at all* |
| NotebookLM | Not a search engine | **Not reading the pile** |
| Midjourney | Not stock photo sites | **Not commissioning anything** |
| Khanmigo | Not a textbook | **Not getting tutored** |
| Lovable | Not an IDE | **Not building the thing** |

**Consequence for competitive analysis:** a market map of the incumbent category
will understate the opportunity by an order of magnitude, because the largest
segment isn't on it. And it explains category-defying growth rates — you are not
taking share, you are converting abstention.

## B4. AI manufactures second-order jobs

Here is where orthodoxy needs a careful amendment.

Jobs are stable — **first-order** jobs. But when a product serves a first-order
job by making production nearly free, it **creates circumstances that did not
previously exist**, and new circumstances generate new jobs.

| First-order job served | Second-order job created |
| --- | --- |
| Generate the text/code/image | **"Help me verify output I didn't produce"** |
| Produce forty options instantly | **"Help me choose"** — selection was never a job when options were scarce |
| Let the system act on my behalf | **"Help me undo what it did"** |
| Answer from my documents | **"Help me tell whether that came from my sources"** |
| Draft in my name | **"Help me make sure it still sounds like me"** |

These are real jobs with real outcome statements, and they did not exist before.

> **This is the honest amendment to Christensen for the AI era: first-order jobs
> are stable; AI manufactures second-order jobs, and the second-order jobs are
> where the product craft now lives.**

Verification surfaces, citations, diffs, checkpoints, undo, "sounds like me" —
every one of these is a product serving a job its own category created. That's
not a footnote to the framework. It's where the next decade of product work is.

## B5. The outcome statements change shape

Pre-AI, desired outcomes were dominated by **speed and effort**:

- Minimize the time it takes to produce a draft
- Minimize the effort required to format a document

Post-AI, a second family becomes dominant — **trust and fidelity**:

- Minimize the **likelihood that the output is wrong in a way I can't detect**
- Minimize the **time it takes to verify** the output
- Minimize the **likelihood that the output doesn't sound like me**
- Minimize the **effort required to recover** from a wrong output

**Why this matters practically:** speed outcomes saturate. Once a thing takes two
seconds, making it one second wins nothing. Trust outcomes do not saturate — and
they are where every AI product still has room. A roadmap made only of speed
outcomes in 2026 is a roadmap into a wall.

## B6. The job executor changes

Ulwick's "job executor" is the person who performs the job. AI-native products
systematically **change who that is**:

| Job | Executor before | Executor now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Produce a research synthesis | Research assistant | The principal |
| Produce a legal first draft | Associate | Partner |
| Produce an illustration | Designer | The person with the idea |
| Produce a working prototype | Engineer | The person with the problem |

This is not just labour saving. **The value is that context stops leaking at the
handoff.** When you delegate, the specialist never quite knows what you meant and
you never quite know what they assumed. Removing the handoff removes that loss.

**And it changes which outcomes matter**, because a partner cares about different
outcomes than an associate does. When the executor changes, re-derive the outcome
statements from scratch — inheriting the old ones is the most common error here.

# Part C — Pre- and post-AI-native JTBD framing, side by side

| | **Pre-gen-AI framing** | **AI-native framing** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The job** | Stable | **Still stable.** Do not let anyone tell you AI created new first-order jobs. |
| **What moved** | — | **The acceptable outcome.** The success threshold ratcheted up. |
| **Which force you compete on** | Pull — more capability, more accuracy, more features | **Anxiety** — cost of trying, cost of being wrong, cost of learning |
| **How much of the job map you serve** | One or two steps | **Steps on both sides**, especially Prepare and Conclude |
| **Who you are taking share from** | The category incumbent | **Non-consumption**, mostly |
| **Dominant outcome family** | Speed and effort — *and these saturate* | **Trust and fidelity — and these don't** |
| **Who executes the job** | A specialist | **The person who holds the context** |
| **Second-order jobs** | Few; solutions were slow enough that scarcity managed itself | **Many, and they are the product craft** — verify, select, undo, sound-like-me |
| **What "good" means** | Output I can work with | **Output I can use unedited** |
| **What the product asks of the user** | Adapt to the machine — learn it, train it, format for it | **Nothing. The machine adapts.** |

## The one-line version

> **Pre-gen-AI: the user met the machine's standard.**
> **Post-gen-AI: the machine meets the user's standard.**
>
> In JTBD terms — the motivation held constant, the expected outcome rose, and
> the work of closing the gap moved from the user's side of the job map to the
> product's.

# Part D — Wispr Flow, written strictly

## The job

**Job story:**

> **When** I have a formed thought and an empty text field in front of me,
> **I want to** get it out of my head at something near the speed I think it,
> **so I can** stay in the flow of the actual work instead of becoming my own
> transcriptionist.

**Functional job:** convert an internal thought into external, usable text.
**Emotional job:** not experience myself as the bottleneck to my own thinking.
**Social job:** **produce text that reads as competent** — nobody should be able
to tell it was dictated.

The social job is the one every prior solution failed, and the one nobody wrote
down.

## Desired outcome statements — and who served them

| Outcome statement | Pre-gen-AI | Post-gen-AI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimize the time it takes to get a formed thought into a text field | Partly served | Served |
| **Minimize the effort required to structure a thought before expressing it** | **Actively made worse** | **Served** |
| Minimize the likelihood that output needs editing before it can be sent | Unserved | Served |
| **Minimize the likelihood the recipient can tell it was dictated** | **Unserved** | **Served** |
| Minimize the time it takes to correct a misrecognized word | Poorly served | Served |
| Minimize the effort to adapt the same content to a different destination | Unserved | Served |
| Minimize the setup effort before first useful output | Unserved — voice enrollment, custom dictionary | Served — zero setup |

**Read the second row again.** Old dictation didn't merely fail to serve that
outcome; it made it *worse than typing*. It demanded you compose finished prose
in your head and then perform it aloud — which is precisely the cognitive labour
the job exists to avoid.

**That is the entire forty-year failure, stated as one underserved outcome.**

## The competitive set

| Competitor | Share of the job |
| --- | --- |
| Typing | Dominant |
| **Not writing it down at all** | **Large, and invisible on any market map** |
| A human assistant | Small; priced out of reach |
| Voice memos transcribed later | Marginal |
| Dictation software | **Near zero, despite four decades and free distribution** |

## Four forces: why the switch didn't happen, and then did

**Pre-gen-AI**

| Force | Strength | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Push | **Strong** | I think faster than I type; my hands hurt; the day is full of typing |
| Pull | *Moderate* | Speech is ~3× faster — true, but theoretical |
| **Anxiety** | **Very strong** | Will it understand me? Must I train it? Will I look ridiculous talking to my laptop? If the output is garbage I've lost the time twice |
| **Habit** | **Very strong** | I've typed for twenty-five years. It costs me no conscious thought. |

**Push + Pull < Anxiety + Habit → no switch, for forty years.**

**Post-gen-AI**

| Force | Strength | What changed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Push | Strong | Unchanged |
| **Pull** | **Very strong** | Not merely faster — *finished*. Often better than I'd have typed. |
| **Anxiety** | **Collapsed** | No training, no enrollment, free tier, hold-to-talk (no ambient mic, no privacy dread), works inside the app I'm already in, and a failed attempt costs me one retype |
| Habit | Strong but confronted | Daily visible social proof — colleagues doing it in front of me |

**Push + Pull > Anxiety + Habit → switch.**

> **The product lesson, derived rather than asserted:** every competitor spent
> forty years pushing on **Pull**. Wispr won by demolishing **Anxiety**. Look at
> the roadmap through that lens and it stops looking like a feature list —
> hold-to-talk, zero setup, the free tier, living inside the app you're already
> in, passive vocabulary learning. **Every one of those is an anxiety reduction,
> not a capability increase.**

## Second-order jobs Wispr created

Comparatively few, which is part of why it is such a clean case — but not zero:

- *"Help me dictate where other people can hear me"* → hold-to-talk, and the
  reason Canto (noisy-environment speech model) exists
- *"Help me trust it with confidential text"* → SOC 2, SSO, HIPAA; the enterprise
  gate is a second-order job made into a product tier

# Part E — The portfolio, held to the same discipline

Each candidate as a job story, the outcome that was previously underserved, and
the second-order job it created. **The test of a good job statement: strike the
product name and it should still read as true, and as old.**

### Wispr Flow
> When I have a formed thought and an empty text field, I want to get it out at
> the speed I think, so I can stay in the work rather than transcribing myself.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the effort to structure a thought *before*
expressing it. **Second-order job:** dictating where others can hear you.

### Descript
> When I have raw footage and a point to make, I want to shape it without
> learning a timeline editor, so I can publish while the idea is still relevant.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the skill required to make a small edit.
**Second-order job:** *"help me fix one misspoken word without re-recording."*

### Granola
> When I'm in a meeting where I must both participate and remember, I want a
> record of what *I* judged important, so I can act on it later without
> re-listening.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the likelihood the record doesn't reflect my
own judgement. **Second-order job:** *"keep it sounding like me"* — which is the
whole product.

### NotebookLM / Gemini Notebook
> When I'm accountable for a body of material I haven't time to read, I want to
> interrogate it as if I had, so I can make a decision I'm able to defend.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the time to find where sources disagree.
**Second-order job:** *"let me check that came from my documents"* → citations.

### Cursor
> When I know what the code should do but not the exact syntax or API, I want to
> describe the change and see it, so I can spend attention on judgement rather
> than typing.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the time to go from intent to a working change.
**Second-order job:** *"help me review more code than I can read"* → the diff
view, which is now the product.

### Midjourney
> When there's an image in my head I can neither draw nor describe precisely, I
> want to iterate toward it, so I can get something usable without commissioning
> anyone.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the cost of the fortieth attempt.
**Second-order job:** *"help me choose among forty options"* — selection was
never a job when options were scarce.

### Lovable / Replit Agent
> When I have an idea for a tool and no engineer, I want to see it working, so I
> can find out whether I actually want it.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the cost of discovering the idea was wrong.
**Second-order job:** *"help me undo something I don't understand"* →
checkpoints.

### Khanmigo
> When I'm stuck and there's nobody to ask, I want someone patient to get me
> unstuck, so I can keep going without being handed the answer.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the likelihood I'm given the answer when I
needed the method. Note it is a *minimize-the-likelihood-of-help* outcome — rare,
and the reason the product is engineered to refuse.

### Duolingo conversational practice
> When I want to practise speaking and I'm embarrassed to be bad in front of a
> person, I want a partner who won't judge me, so I can build fluency without
> the social cost.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the social risk of practising badly. **A purely
emotional outcome** — and the clearest proof that the scarce input was social,
not informational.

### Google Lens / Circle to Search
> When I'm looking at something I can't name, I want to search for it directly,
> so I can find out what it is without guessing at words.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the effort of translating the world into a
query.

### Gamma
> When I have an argument to make and a deadline, I want a structure to react to,
> so I can spend my time on the thinking rather than the formatting.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the time from having a point to having
something to react to. **Note "react to" — the job is not "make me a deck."**

### Perplexity
> When I need a defensible answer quickly, I want the answer and its sources
> together, so I can use it without doing the research twice.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the time it takes to verify an answer.
**Rare case: a product whose core job *is* a second-order job.**

### Spotify AI DJ
> When I want music and don't want to choose, I want something to decide and tell
> me why, so I can feel accompanied rather than served a list.

**Underserved outcome:** minimize the likelihood I feel like I'm being fed an
algorithm. Purely emotional — and the reason the *narration* is the product.

# How to run this in class

If JTBD is the frame, restructure the twenty minutes around it:

| Minutes | Segment |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Snapshot. Wispr: founded 2021, pivoted mid-2024, $2B Aug 2026. |
| 2–4 | Live demo — messy sentence with a self-correction, into Slack, then email. |
| 4–7 | **The job statement.** All three layers. Establish it's forty years old. |
| 7–11 | **The four forces, pre-gen-AI.** Land why the switch never happened. Then post. Let them see it was Anxiety, not Pull. |
| 11–14 | **The job map.** Four of seven steps moved from user to product. |
| 14–17 | **Second-order jobs.** What AI creates by succeeding. |
| 17–20 | Part C contrast table + the three questions for their own products. |

**The single question to leave them with:**

> For a job in your domain that everyone agrees is real and that nothing gets
> hired for — **which of the four forces has been blocking it, and has generative
> AI just moved that one?**
