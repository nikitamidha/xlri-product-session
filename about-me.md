## Coaching

I'm a **trained coach, certified through UC Berkeley** — `[[FILL: exact
programme name and year, e.g. UC Berkeley Executive Coaching Institute, 2023]]`.

I went in expecting a credential and came out with a working habit: **ask one
more question before offering the answer.** Coaching training is mostly
unlearning the reflex to solve. You learn to sit in someone else's framing long
enough to find the thing they haven't said yet — which is, almost always, the
actual problem.

That turns out to be the same muscle product management runs on. A user
interview and a coaching conversation fail for identical reasons: you heard a
request and treated it as a requirement. You solved the sentence instead of the
situation. Jobs to be Done is, read one way, just coaching discipline applied to
a market — *don't take the stated want at face value; find what they're
actually trying to get done.*

So it isn't a side interest sitting next to the product work. It's the thing
underneath it.

## A small insight about me

I'm most useful at the point where a team has agreed on **what** to build and
nobody has said out loud **why anyone would hire it.** That gap is where almost
all wasted quarters live, and it's rarely a strategy failure — it's a listening
failure. Everyone heard the request. Nobody stayed in the room long enough to
hear the job.

Which is why I teach it the way I do: not as a framework to apply, but as a
question to stay inside of a little longer than is comfortable.
